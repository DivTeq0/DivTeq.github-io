    Section 1.3 Solutions

    An analytic or closed form solution to a differential equation is an expression relating the dependent and independent variables without derivatives, which when differentiated makes the differential equation an identity. Solutions can given in explicit form \(y=f(t)\) or in implicit form \(g(y,t)=\text{constant}\)
    🔗

    Subsection 1.3.1 Analytic solutions in explicit form

    If the differential equation is
    \begin{equation*} y'=f(t,y) \end{equation*}
    then an explicit solution \(y=g(t)\) of the equation makes
    \begin{equation*} g'(t)=f(t,g(t)) \end{equation*}
    an identity
    🔗

    Example 1.3.1.

    The differential equation
    \begin{equation*} y'=-\frac{3}{t}\,y+6t^2 \end{equation*}
    has a solution \(y=t^3\) since
    \begin{equation*} (t^3)'=-\frac{3t^3}{t}+6t^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3t^2=-3t^2+6t^2 \end{equation*}
    So it satisfies the equation. \(y=t^3\) is called a particular solution. The general solution to the equation is
    \begin{equation*} y=t^3+\frac{c}{t^3} \end{equation*}
    where \(c\) is an arbitrary constant analogous to the \(+C\) when finding antiderivatives
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.3.2 Analytic solutions in implicit form

    To check whether an implicit function \(g(t,y)=\text{constant}\) satisfies a differential equation
    \begin{equation*} y'=f(t,y) \end{equation*}
    we have to implicitly differentiate the function and solve for \(y'\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{d}{dt}(g(t,y))=\partial_t g +\partial_y g\, y'=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad y'=-\frac{g_t}{g_y} \end{equation*}
    \(g_t,g_y\) are the partial derivatives of g, that is they are the usual derivatives with respect to the variable while considering all the other variables to be constant. The function will be a solution to the equation if
    \begin{equation*} -\frac{g_t}{g_y}=f(t,y) \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Example 1.3.2.

    A closed form solution to the differential equation
    \begin{equation*} y'=-\frac{\cos y+2ty}{-t\sin y+t^2+3y^2} \end{equation*}
    is given by \(g(t,y)=t\cos y+t^2y+y^3=c\) since
    \begin{equation*} \partial_t g=\cos y+2ty\qquad \partial_y g=-t\sin y+t^2+3y^2 \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation*} -\frac{g_t}{g_y}=-\frac{\cos y+2ty}{-t\sin y+t^2+3y^2}=f(t,y) \end{equation*}
