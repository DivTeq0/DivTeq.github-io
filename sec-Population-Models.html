    Section 2.3 Population Models

    Subsection 2.3.1 Exponential Growth

    The simplest population model states that the rate of growth of a population is proportional to the population. The associated differential equation is
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dp}{dt}=rp \qquad p(0)=p_0 \end{equation*}
    It is separable
    \begin{equation*} \int\frac{dp}{p}=\int r\,dt \quad\Rightarrow\quad \ln p=rt+c \end{equation*}
    Using the initial condition gives \(c=\ln p_0\text{.}\) An the solution is
    \begin{equation*} p(t)=p_0e^{rt}=p_0(e^r)^t \end{equation*}
    It isn’t a good for long term behavior as the population will become unbounded eventually
    🔗

    Example 2.3.1.

    A bacterial colony is found to have 2000 individuals after 2 hours. After 5 hours the colony has grown to 54000 individuals
    🔗
    (a) What was the initial population?
    🔗
    (b) At what time will the colony contain a million individuals?
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} p(2)=2000=p_0e^{2r}\qquad p(5)=5400=p_0e^{5r} \end{equation*}
    Dividing
    \begin{equation*} \frac{54000}{2000}=e^{3r}\quad \Rightarrow \quad e^r=3 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} p(t)=p_0(3^t)\quad 2000=p_0(3^2)\quad\Rightarrow\quad p_0=\frac{2000}{9} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 1000000=\frac{2000}{9}\,3^t\quad\Rightarrow\quad t=\frac{\ln 45000}{\ln 3}\approx7.66 \,\mbox{hours} \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Subsection 2.3.2 The Logistic equation

    A Belgian biologist called Verhulst proposed a modification of exponential growth
    \begin{equation*} p-=rp\left(1-\frac{p}{m}\right) \end{equation*}
    where \(r.m\) are given constants. The idea is that for small values of \(p\) the population grows exponentially. As \(p\) increases there is a resistance to growth modeled by the \(-rp^2/m\) term. This is an attempt to take into account the struggle for resources as the population increases.
    🔗
    The equation is separable
    \begin{equation*} \int \frac{dp}{p(p-m)} = -\int \frac{r}{m}\,dt \end{equation*}
    Using partial fractions gives
    \begin{equation*} \frac{1}{m} \int \left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p-m}\right)\,dp = -\int \frac{r}{m}\,dt \end{equation*}
    Integrating gives
    \begin{equation*} -\ln p +\ln\left|p-m\right| = -rt + c_1 \end{equation*}
    The initial condition \(p(0)=p_0\) implies that
    \begin{equation*} c_1=\ln \left|\frac{p_0-m}{p_0}\right| \end{equation*}
    Integrating leads to
    \begin{equation*} \ln\left|\frac{p-m}{p}\right|=-rt+\ln \left|\frac{p_0-m}{p_0}\right|\quad\Rightarrow\quad 1-\frac{m}{p}=\left(1-\frac{m}{p_0}\right)e^{-rt} \end{equation*}
    Solving for \(p\) gives
    \begin{equation*} p(t) = \frac{mp_0}{p_0+(m-p_0)e^{-rt}} \end{equation*}
    The constant \(r\) is called the growth rate by analogy with the simple exponential growth. \(m\) is called the carrying capacity since
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{t\to\infty}p(t)=m \end{equation*}

    Subsection 2.3.3 The Extinction-Explosion (Doomsday) Model

    A slightly different model is given by the DE
    \begin{equation*} p'=rp\left(\frac{p}{ m}-1\right) \end{equation*}
    A similar analysis leads to the expression for \(p\) as
    \begin{equation*} p(t) = \frac{mp_0}{p_0+(m-p_0)e^{rt}} \end{equation*}
    If \(p_0 \lt m\) then
    \begin{equation*} m-p_0 \gt 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \lim_{t\to\infty}p(t)=0 \end{equation*}
    If \(p_0\gt m\) then \(m-p_0 \lt 0\) and the denominator becomes zero at a finite value of \(t\)
    \begin{equation*} p_0+(m-p_0)e^{rt}=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad t=\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{p_0}{p_0-m}\right) \end{equation*}
